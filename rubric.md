# Technica Hackathon Rubric

A quick reference for the supplied challenges and judging criteria. **Official scoring scales and weights were not provided.**

## 1. Challenge Prompts

### STR — Sensors for Good
- **Goal:** Use sensor data to protect people or places (personal safety, environmental protection, etc.).
- **Questions:** Who or what are we protecting? What sensor data do we use? How does that data lead to meaningful protection?
- **Prize:** 1 winner; $1,000 total in gift cards split between teammates.

### Capital One — Nessie API
- **Goal:** Reimagine banking with an innovative application using Nessie's mock financial data: accounts, merchants, bills, and peer-to-peer transactions.
- **Questions:** What real financial problem are we solving? Which Nessie endpoints power the solution? How does it empower users or improve their financial lives?
- **Prize:** Not specified in the supplied prompt.

### Next-Generation Finance — ETFs, AI & Digital Assets
- **Goal:** Build a prototype, tool, experience, or concept addressing a real financial problem using **one or more** of these technologies:
  - **ETFs:** Compare funds, fees, exposures, strategies, risks, or portfolio choices.
  - **Digital assets/tokenization:** Demonstrate issuance, transfers, settlement, custody, or tracking.
  - **AI:** Support research, personalization, risk discovery, education, fraud awareness, or workflow efficiency.
- **Key questions:**
  1. What user need or financial problem does the solution address?
  2. How is the technology applied in a realistic financial setting?
  3. What risks, limitations, or adoption challenges should users understand?
  4. How does the solution build trust through transparency, accessibility, and responsible design?
- **Example directions:** An AI-enabled ETF allocation simulator or a tokenized-asset lifecycle simulator.
- **Prize:** 4 portable monitors.

## 2. General Technica Judging

| Criterion | Questions to answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Adherence to Technica's Mission** | Does the project support inclusion through accessibility, representation, safety, or empowerment? This evaluates the project, not teammates' identities. |
| **UI/UX Design** | Is the experience visually appealing, intuitive, and easy to use? |
| **Functionality & Execution** | Does the project work as intended? Can we demonstrate its core functionality? |
| **Originality & Creativity** | What makes the idea or execution creative or distinctive? |
| **Adherence to Category** | How directly does the project address the category we enter? |

## 3. Additional Next-Generation Finance Judging

These criteria are specific to the finance prompt; they are not stated as requirements for every challenge.

| Criterion | Questions to answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Impact & Application** | Is the financial use case meaningful, with practical value for users? |
| **Innovation** | Is the idea creative, forward-looking, and relevant to next-generation finance? |
| **Clarity & Accuracy** | Are financial and technical concepts explained accurately, without overstating benefits or minimizing risks? |
| **Accessibility** | Can people with different levels of financial knowledge understand and use it? |
| **Feasibility & Responsible Design** | Is implementation realistic? Have we addressed privacy, security, bias, regulation, transparency, and human oversight? |

## 4. Suggested Team Self-Grading

**Internal planning aid only—not an official scoring system.** Rate each applicable criterion:

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Missing or unclear. |
| 2 | Partially addressed; major gaps remain. |
| 3 | Clearly addressed with a basic example or demo. |
| 4 | Well executed, with convincing evidence. |
| 5 | Exceptionally strong, polished, and supported by evidence. |

For each rating, note **one piece of evidence** and **one next improvement**. Prioritize weak areas and prepare a short demo showing the user problem, working solution, impact, and limitations.
